Follow along

Every screen below, and where it hides in the demo

Nothing on this page is a mock-up — but almost none of it is on screen when you arrive. A new room opens on an empty world with a build toolbar, and everything else lives behind two buttons in the top bar. Open the demo beside this page and work down the list.

Press Connect on the card that greets you — the name, room and password are already filled in. You are on your own in there, which also makes you the host, so every control below is yours to press.

The world standing on real streets

Worlds & room → Pin to my location, or type coordinates and press Travel here → pick a Ground (Coastline or Ground & sea) → Draw this place. The strip at the top of the picture above names the place and the scale it was drawn at.

The eighteen maps, and the fifty real places

Worlds & room → scroll to Load a map. Three tabs: Built in · 18, Real places · 50, and Saved here, which fills up with whatever this room has kept. Below them, Or make one builds a map From a picture, From a real place or From a save.

The scale ladder

Worlds & room → the dropdown beside Pin to my location. Eight rungs, from Street · 1 m per block up to Planet · 250 km. The world stays 161 × 161 blocks at every rung — only what a block means changes.

A photo turned into a building

Worlds & room → Or make one → the From a picture card. Brightness becomes height, so a light sky and a dark skyline come out as a ridge line.

The twelve modes

Play in the top bar. The picker lists all twelve with rounds, blueprint source and round time along the top. Six run with one player: Blueprint Race, Where on Earth, Team Build, Memory Match, Earthquake and Demolition Party. Voxel Charades and Territory need two. Relay Sculptors, Postcard, Saboteur and Block Rush need three — the picker greys those out and says “Needs 3+ players — you have 1” rather than letting you start something that cannot finish. That is the likeliest reason a mode on this page looks missing.

Getting a second and third player

Share copies the room link. Open it in another tab, another window, or on your phone — each one joins as a separate player, which is enough to unlock every mode. No account, no install.

Memory Match and Postcard, round by round

Play → Memory Match (works alone) or Postcard (needs three, because the room votes and you cannot vote for yourself). The scoreboards further down this page are what appears when the round ends.

The time-lapse

Worlds & room → Time-lapse → Play the time-lapse. It replays the room from whatever it looked like when you arrived, on your screen only — build something first or it has nothing to show.

The map under your feet

Map (or press N) opens the minimap, with the coastline, the lat/lon grid, what has been built and where everyone is standing. Walk (or G) drops you into the world in first person to walk it.

The ground

The map is under your feet, not in the corner

Anchor a world and the floor knows where it is: the real coastline, or the real streets, parks and building footprints, stitched from map tiles at that world's own scale — and carried out across the plain that runs nine worlds further in every direction.

  • Plain, coastline or streets — your choice, and it is a picture rather than a hundred thousand cubes to clear
  • Draw this place turns those footprints into blocks you can actually build on
  • Zoom is the scale ladder: a metre a block up to 250 km, each rung its own saved world
  • The minimap stays offline on purpose — it draws its coastline from a file that ships with the game

Scale

Zooming out means a block covers more ground

The camera has a limit, and past it "see more" stops being a camera move: a world is one tile of the Earth at one scale, which is exactly why two rooms standing in the same place see the same world. So zoom is a ladder, and every rung is its own saved world — move in a rung and your street is still a street, not a shrunken county.

Every world is 161 × 161 blocks and 40 high, at every rung. The size is part of the address.

How big a block is161 × 161 either way
  • 1 m Street 161 m across
  • 2 m Block 322 m across
  • 5 m Neighbourhood 805 m across
  • 20 m District 3.2 km across
  • 100 m City 16 km across
  • 1 km Region 161 km across
  • 20 km Continent 3,220 km across
  • 250 km Planet the whole Earth

Bring your own

Point at a photo, get a building

Drop in a picture and BlockParty reads its shape as well as its colours: the background is flood-filled away, depth is read off the brightness, and every column is pushed back by how far away it looks. What you get is a painted shell you can walk around.

  • Five styles: mural, mosaic, relief, solid relief, or terrain read as a heightfield
  • True colour, or dithered down to the twelve you build with
  • It never leaves your browser, and it arrives as one edit — so one undo takes it back out
  • One photograph knows the front of a thing. Walk behind it and it is a shell — that is the honest ceiling

A round of Memory Match

Study it, lose it, draw it back

One player builds while the room watches. Then it is taken away and everybody rebuilds it from memory — and at the end you find out which part you actually got wrong.

1 · Watch

Somebody builds it

The architect ends their own turn when they are done, so nobody watches an empty stage.

2 · Remember

Then it is gone

A flashback outlines the shape mid-round — no colours. Remembering what shape it was stops being the whole game.

3 · Compare

What you got wrong

place 88% shape 64% colour 41%

The score was always these three things. This is the first time you get to see which one cost you.

One of the twelve pictures, full size: sixteen squares across, in the same twelve colours you build with — small enough that a late joiner gets it for free, and coarse enough that what it shows remains your call.

Postcard · new

One picture. No model to copy.

Everyone gets the same picture and nothing else — no ghost, no blueprint, no cell-for-cell target. Work out what it shows, build that in three dimensions, and the room votes for the one it likes best. What the picture is called is only revealed at the end.

  • Twelve pictures ship with the game
  • Plots stay covered until the reveal
  • Three players, because you cannot vote for yourself
